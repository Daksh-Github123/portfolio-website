2026 · The Orcas

The Orca

CTO & Arm Design Lead

Our 2026 underwater ROV, built in about two months for ~$600, against rival budgets of $15,000–20,000.

At a glance

Flight controllerPixhawk 2.4.8
FirmwareArduSub (Sub V4.5.7)
Configured inMission Planner 1.3.83
Frame configurationVectored 6-DOF (FRAME_CONFIG = 2)
Propulsion6 thrusters
ManipulatorSingle-motor gripper
Profiling floatESP32 · ESP-NOW · syringe buoyancy engine
Build time / cost~2 months · ~$600

How it works

Mechanical

The manipulator is a single-motor gripper. Its gripping surface is a kitchen sponge combined with M-seal epoxy putty, and we replaced the putty after every run.

Electronics and control

A Pixhawk 2.4.8 flight controller runs ArduSub (Sub V4.5.7), configured in Mission Planner 1.3.83 for the vectored 6-DOF frame (FRAME_CONFIG = 2) and its six thrusters. We flashed the Pixhawk using Mission Planner's Force Bootloader option.

Profiling float

A NEMO-style vertical profiling float changes depth with a syringe-based buoyancy engine, with a DC-thruster version as the backup. It runs on an ESP32 and communicates over ESP-NOW.

Design decisions

  1. Sponge plus M-seal epoxy putty as the gripping surface

    Why
    3D-printed jaws, sponge alone and putty alone each failed underwater.
    Trade-off
    The putty had to be replaced after every run.

  2. A DC-thruster version of the float's buoyancy engine as backup

    Why
    A second way to drive the float if the syringe engine failed.

What broke and what we changed

  • Manipulator: we planned a worm-gear jaw with wrist rotation, then dropped it for time and built the single-motor gripper instead.
  • Servo waterproofing stayed a recurring problem: too much sealant jammed the gears, and too little let water in.
  • Float link: we replaced the float's earlier Bluetooth link with ESP-NOW.

My role

  • As Arm Design Lead, designed and built The Orca's single-motor gripper.

Result: India national champions with a national-record 280/375 points; Sharkpedo Legacy Award at the 2026 World Championship.

2026 · Independent research project

BLADEBotanical Leaf Analysis & Disease Evaluator

Lead Engineer & Co-researcher

A handheld, fully offline crop-disease scanner that classifies a leaf photo, or answers "uncertain" instead of guessing.

At a glance

ComputerRaspberry Pi 4B
Camera5 MP OV5647
InterfaceTouchscreen GUI
PowerBattery
EnclosureCustom 3D-printed
ConnectivityFully offline
ModelMobileNetV2, pretrained on PlantVillage
Classes38 (26 diseases, 12 healthy) · 14 crops
Inference5-view test-time augmentation
LoggingEvery scan to a spreadsheet

How it works

Hardware

A Raspberry Pi 4B with a 5 MP OV5647 camera, a touchscreen GUI and a battery, in a custom 3D-printed enclosure. Everything runs offline.

Software pipeline

Input checks run first: a plant-colour check, an object filter, and a face/privacy filter. A pretrained MobileNetV2 (trained on PlantVillage) then classifies the leaf into 38 classes (26 diseases, 12 healthy states) across 14 crops, combining five augmented views of each image (test-time augmentation). Confidence and margin thresholds decide whether to show a diagnosis or answer "uncertain". Every scan is logged to a spreadsheet.

Study design

The study photographs the same physical leaves while changing one variable at a time: background, framing and lighting. It compares lab-to-field accuracy (PlantVillage → PlantDoc) and measures image statistics: colour saturation, border edge density and leaf fill fraction.

Design decisions

  1. Confidence and margin thresholds that can answer "uncertain"

    Why
    A wrong diagnosis is worse than no diagnosis.
    Trade-off
    Some scans end without a diagnosis.

  2. Input checks before the model sees the image

    Why
    Only plant images reach the classifier, and the face filter protects privacy.

  3. Five augmented views per image (test-time augmentation)

    Trade-off
    Five model passes per scan instead of one.

What broke and what we changed

  • The original camera's board shifted inside the case. I swapped the camera and reworked the internal layout.
  • I ran a full software audit and put the code under Git version control with a restore point.
  • Speed: I added per-stage timing, moved logging to the background and batched inference. Quantized TFLite is in progress.

My role

  • Rebuilt the device: camera swap and a reworked internal layout.
  • Audited the software, added Git version control, instrumented per-stage timing and optimized speed.
  • Redesigned the study as a controlled experiment.

Result: Paper under review.

2024, 2025 · Team Yo_Zephyrus (2024) · Team Huayra (2025)

WRO Future Engineers self-driving car

Team Member (2024) · Team Lead (2025)

The task

The car must be fully autonomous: no remote control, and radios and Wi-Fi off during runs. It starts with a single button press.

The arena is a 3 × 3 m track with walls. Open Challenge: 3 laps with a randomized driving direction, starting position and inner-wall placement, without touching the walls.

Obstacle Challenge: 3 laps around red and green pillars (pass red on the right, green on the left). The last pillar of lap 2 decides whether lap 3 continues or reverses direction. Then the car finds a magenta-marked parking lot and parallel parks. Moving a pillar costs points, and each run has a 3-minute limit.

Vehicle limits: 300 × 200 × 300 mm and 1.5 kg; four wheels, one driving axle and one steering actuator. Car-style steering is required; differential-drive steering is banned.

Source: WRO Future Engineers General Rules ↗

At a glance

CarsZephyrus (2024) · Huayra (2025)
ComputerRaspberry Pi
VisionOpenCV colour detection (Python)
DetectionContour analysis · size-threshold filtering
SteeringAckermann
DriveRear drive motor
DifferentialLego (2024) · custom, from an RC truck (2025)

How it works

Vision

Python with OpenCV colour detection finds the red and green pillars, using contour analysis and size-threshold filtering.

Drivetrain

Ackermann steering and a rear drive motor. 2024 used a store-bought Lego differential; in 2025 we built a custom differential from a disassembled RC truck and its single large motor.

Design decisions

  1. Ackermann steering

    Why
    The rules require car-style steering with one steering actuator; differential-drive steering is banned.

  2. Contour analysis with size-threshold filtering on colour detections

    Why
    Pillars are identified by colour, and the size threshold drops detections too small to be a pillar.

What broke and what we changed

  • 2024 → 2025: replaced the store-bought Lego differential with a custom differential built from a disassembled RC truck and its single large motor.
  • Duct tape proved too weak to hold the custom differential, so a teammate modelled custom structural supports in CAD.

My role

  • 2024 (team member): mostly physical work, including soldering and testing.
  • 2025 (team lead): led the 3-person team through the custom-differential rebuild.
  • Did most of the design and strategy for the 2025 car.

Result: 19th of 40+ at the 2025 nationals.
