I discovered robotics in ninth grade through my strengths in physics and computer science. My early exposure to competitive robotics showed me how complex real systems can be, and it quickly became clear that I needed a deeper grasp of the fundamentals — so I deliberately stepped back from competitions to strengthen my foundation through structured coursework and hands-on experimentation, building small projects to understand electronics, software, and sensing from the ground up.

Since then, I've applied those fundamentals to increasingly complex projects — autonomous vehicles, underwater robotics, and now agricultural sensing — often taking ownership of system design and software development. I've developed a strong preference for learning by building, iterating, and testing ideas in the real world rather than relying purely on theory.

I'm now transitioning from competition-based projects to a large-scale independent robotics project — currently co-authoring an IEEE paper on plant disease detection and soil sensing (BLADE) — while documenting my work publicly to build an engineering track record. Long-term, I want to study engineering at a rigorous academic institution and use that foundation to build hardware-driven ventures.
